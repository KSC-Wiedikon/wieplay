# Spike S0: off-the-shelf models on our footage (16.10.2026)

PLAN §17 S0, run on 16.10.2026 on footage the club already has: H3's games from Luca Zbinden's
YouTube channel (cleared for training, PLAN §11). Nothing was trained or fine-tuned; every
model ran as downloaded. Scripts: [`spikes/s0/`](../../spikes/s0/README.md). Evidence (crop
sheets, previews, CSVs, spectrograms) is on the NAS in `wieplay/datasets/youtube-h3/spike-s0/`;
it shows players, so it stays out of the repo.

## Summary

- **No training from scratch.** Two open-source volleyball ball trackers already work on our
  footage as downloaded. **VballNet-Fast** (MIT) is the baseline: it finds the most balls in
  every hall, and when it reports a ball it is right 18–20 times out of 20.
- **Ball tracking needs no GPU.** VballNet-Fast runs at 125 frames/s on the Lenovo's CPU
  (1,100 on the laptop GPU). A 2 h match at 30 fps is about 30 min of CPU, or about 10 min for
  the live part only. The GPU budget (§10.9) is left for player detection and fine-tuning.
- **Player detection works unchanged** (YOLO11m, COCO weights), far court included.
- **Whistles are a good signal in league games but not enough alone.** A friendly without a
  referee has none, and a tournament hall has the neighbouring courts' whistles too.
- **What fine-tuning has to fix:** distant or very wide-angle cameras (SUC, Wetzikon), where
  the second tracker (WASB) falls to 25–58 % of VballNet-Fast's finds. The ball-click labels
  of §10.4 should come from those halls first.

## Ball tracking

Candidates (all checked for licence before use):

| Model | Licence | Trained on | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| [VballNet](https://github.com/asigatchov/vball-net) V1 and Fast (TrackNetV4-based, ONNX, 50–170 KB) | MIT | indoor volleyball from behind the back line | |
| [WASB](https://github.com/nttcom/WASB-SBDT) (HRNet) | MIT | Volleyball dataset (broadcast view) | BMVC 2023 |
| [TrackNetV3](https://github.com/qaz812345/TrackNetV3) with [beach-volley weights](https://huggingface.co/deadfast/beach-volley-vision-models) | MIT | beach volleyball | |
| VolleyVision (YOLO) | CC BY-NC-ND 4.0 | | **not usable**: no derivatives, non-commercial |

Input 512×288, 3 (TrackNet: 8 + background) frames per step, at **30 fps** (every second frame).
At the full 60 fps WASB found fewer balls (21 % of frames instead of 29 %): the models were
trained on 30 fps footage, and the ball moves too little between two 60 fps frames.

**How it was measured, without labels:** share of frames with a detection; share that sits on
the same spot for most of 4 s ("stuck": lamps, lines); share in a plausible track (≥ 8 linked
frames); agreement between two models; **precision by eye** on 20 random detections per model
and clip (a 120 px window of the source frame around each one); and **relative recall**: of
all frames where any of the three has the ball in a track, the share each model finds.

| Clip | Ball in some track | VballNet-Fast: found / precision | WASB | TrackNetV3 beach |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| good-legends | 29 % of frames | 83 % / 18 of 20 | 87 % / 19 of 20 | 54 % / ~13 of 20 (often 30–40 px off) |
| good-stafa | 32 % | 87 % | 63 % | 42 % |
| hard-suc | 17 % | **98 %** / 18 of 20 | 25 % / 19 of 20 | 5 % / 18 of 20 |
| hard-wetzikon | 19 % | **97 %** / 19 of 20 | 58 % / 19 of 20 | 20 % / 19 of 20 |
| quiet-volero (side) | 44 % | 88 % / 20 of 20 | 77 % / 20 of 20 | 15 % / ~19 of 20 |

- Where VballNet-Fast and WASB both see a ball, they agree within 20 px 89–99 % of the time.
  They were trained on different footage, so agreement is a free confidence signal.
- The misses by eye are almost all **shoes** (white, moving, ball-sized) and, in the SUC hall,
  ceiling lamps. Many true detections are a ball **out of play**: held before the serve,
  bounced, rolling, or warm-up balls on other courts. Dead-time logic must not treat "ball
  visible" as "rally".
- **VballNet V1** locks onto one fixed spot (a lamp or ceiling corner) in two of five halls,
  89–98 % of frames. Not usable as is; a court mask would hide it.
- Far court: balls at and beyond the net are among the correct detections in the good halls.
  The two hard halls are where WASB and TrackNet lose most, so they need labels first.
- "Ball in some track" (17–44 % of frames) is in line with Balltime's ~67 % downtime.

Speed on the Framework 16 (RTX 5070 Laptop, 8 GB, ~4 GB free next to another app), batch 8:

| Model | Frames/s | VRAM | 2 h match at 30 fps (216 k frames) |
|---|---|---|---|
| VballNet-Fast (ONNX, GPU) | ~1,100 | small | ~3.5 min |
| VballNet-Fast (ONNX, **Lenovo CPU**, while the worker transcoded) | 125 | – | ~29 min CPU; ~10 min for the live part |
| WASB (FP16) | 336 | 1.0 GB | ~11 min |
| TrackNetV3 beach (FP16) | 664 | 1.6 GB | ~5.5 min |

Decoding 1080p60 and scaling to 512×288 ran at ~1,700 frames/s on the laptop CPU and about
half that on the Lenovo; it is the same order of cost as the model.

## Players

YOLO11m (COCO, AGPL-3.0), one frame per second, 1280 px input, confidence 0.25, no batching:
33–46 frames/s, 14–28 people per frame (players, referees, bench, spectators), smallest boxes
(5th percentile) 35–63 px high. In the SUC hall all 12 players and the referee are found on the
far court. 1920 px input finds slightly more (16 vs 14 per frame) at 32 frames/s.
At 25 fps over the live part of a match (~60 k frames) this is ~25 min unbatched, more than
§10.9's 10–15 min estimate. Batching, a smaller model or a lower rate with the tracker filling
in should close the gap; to measure in M8.

## Whistles

`s0_whistle.py`: per 23 ms, the 2–4.5 kHz band's share of the energy, how far its strongest
bin stands out (tonality) and its level above a 10 s rolling floor; runs of ≥ 0.2 s, parts
< 0.6 s apart joined. CPU only, a few minutes per match.

- League games show clean whistles at 3.6–4.1 kHz, with the expected rhythm: serve whistle,
  ~7 s of play (ball tracks), whistle, pause (timeline sheet on the NAS).
- Counts: 436 in the Volero Legends game (132 min), 361 in Stäfa (80 min), about 20–35 % more
  than two per point; the rest is shoe squeaks and other tonal noise. Warm-up shows as the
  first 11 min without whistles.
- The SUC tournament has whistles at several pitches: ours and the neighbouring courts'. A
  whistle's pitch could tell our referee from the others (cluster by pitch per video).
- The friendly has **no whistles** (no referee), only ball contacts. Games without a referee
  or without sound need the ball and motion signals alone.

## Decisions for the plan

1. §10.4: VballNet-Fast is the baseline ball tracker (MIT), WASB the second opinion; fine-tune
   from VballNet-Fast. Feed 30 fps. Mask the court (stuck spots, other courts' balls).
2. §10.9: ball tracking moves to the Lenovo (CPU, free). The GPU line is for players,
   fine-tuning and later stages.
3. §10.1 v0: rally = ball in a plausible track inside the court mask, with whistles (pitch of
   our referee) and motion as boundaries; no-whistle games fall back to ball + motion.
4. The bar of §10.1 (boundary F1 ≥ 0.9 at ±1 s) needs hand-tagged rallies: 5–10 games, including
   SUC/Wetzikon-type halls and one game without a referee.
5. Not done in S0: a GPU provider benchmark (not needed for ball tracking now), the 4K
   question (the YouTube copies are 1080p; the originals are 4K), and a run over a whole match.
